## The paper's problem

Lemme explain to you in brief terms what the paper is about.

Imagine a bunch of points on a 2D plane, but we can distinguish them by color—some
of them are **red** while others are **blue**. We want to use a circle to _separate_
them according to certain conditions: we want to find the circle with **the smallest**
radius that **encloses all the red points**, and that **contains the smallest
number of blue points**.

Maybe you're saying, "Well... Where's the _dynamic_ part?" Assume we're in the

Of course, you **could** execute the same algo (the static one) again. Now! You're
ready to vanquish every sou... I won't repeat the joke again, you get the point,
don't you?

What if we need to calculate the solution **over and over again**? It turns
out that we can apply a new algo with better time complexity. Thus, we can
retrieve the solution quicker under _insertions_ and _deletions_ of points—we're
solving the problem "dynamically" as new inputs arrive.

## I had to read 29 hrs per second

During the first week, I delivered a hands-on masterclass in ego: _Did the authors
cite another paper to understand why we can assume that there's no **red point**
that doesn't belong to the CH, and that there's no **blue point** inside
the same CH by taking advantage of a Farthest-Point Voronoi Diagram?_ Then
what? Surely a 6th-semester CS student can infer the details under the hood while
reading in the middle of a crowded subway at 6am on Monday.

After one day of thinking I was
<a href="https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2004/6/10/mark-e-zuckerberg-06-the-whiz/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">Mark Zuckerberg in 2004</a>,
I started reading the paper's references.

The most important part was that I needed to read a whole new paper besides
my chosen one. That sounds common, but the thing was that I "needed" to
explain it in class before talking about the main topic. Otherwise, nobody
would understand anything. So, I had 2.5 weeks to prepare "two" presentations.

It was fun. I enjoyed every second of that suffering. I didn't understand
some definitions at first until I drew lots of diagrams. I was so nervous on
presentation day. Yet, I did what I was cut out for (I'll conveniently
ignore my -458 ELO in LoL from playing Ezreal): talking too much. I got
a 9/10.
